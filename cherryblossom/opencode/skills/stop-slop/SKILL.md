## Banned phrases (delete on sight)

These signal AI-generated text. Cut them.

- "In today's fast-paced world of …"
- "Let's dive in" / "Buckle up" / "Stay tuned"
- "It's important to note that …" / "It's worth mentioning …"
- "In conclusion" / "To summarize" (just end; the reader can tell it ended)
- "Furthermore" / "Moreover" / "Additionally" used as filler
- "Game-changer" / "revolutionary" / "cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art"
- "Seamlessly" / "effortlessly" / "robust" / "powerful" (as empty intensifiers)
- "Whether you're a beginner or an expert …"
- "Best practices for X" (when the actual content is generic)
- Lists of three adjectives ("fast, reliable, and scalable")

## Voice rules

1. **Active voice with a human subject.** Not "It was decided to migrate" →
   "We migrated" or "I migrated".
2. **Specific over general.** Not "many issues" → "three issues" or list them.
3. **Concrete examples.** If you make a claim, the next sentence usually shows it.
4. **Cut throat-clearing.** Read your first paragraph; if you can delete the
   first sentence without losing meaning, do it.

## Structure rules

- Don't open with a summary list of what's coming. Just write it.
- Don't end with a recap of what you said. Just stop.
- Don't bold every other phrase. Bold is for the one phrase that matters.
- Lists are for genuinely parallel items. Three sentences in a row beat a bullet
  list of three short fragments.

## Length

Cut. Then cut again. A 600-word post saying one thing well beats a 2000-word
post saying the same thing four ways.

## Self-check before posting

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a marketing email, rewrite. If you can't
identify a specific claim in each paragraph, that paragraph is filler.
